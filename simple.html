About the Journal

SPH Journal (Studies in Policy & Health Economics) is a peer-reviewed, open-access scholarly publication dedicated to advancing rigorous empirical and theoretical research in the fields of health economics, public policy analysis, health systems financing, and socioeconomic determinants of population health. The journal serves as a critical forum for economists, policy researchers, and health systems analysts working at the intersection of economic theory and evidence-based policy formulation.

All manuscripts undergo a double-blind external peer review process administered by an independent editorial committee. Authors are required to submit blinded manuscripts to ensure the integrity and impartiality of the evaluation. The journal adheres to the guidelines established by the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) and maintains strict standards of academic integrity and reproducibility.

Fiscal Multiplier Effects of Public Health Expenditure on Provincial GDP Growth: A Dynamic Panel Analysis of Emerging South Asian Economies, 2005–2024

Muhammad Arslan Khalid, Fatima Zahra Bhatti
Department of Economics, Quaid-i-Azam University, Islamabad, Pakistan
This study examines the heterogeneous fiscal multiplier effects of disaggregated public health expenditure—specifically preventive care allocations, hospital infrastructure investments, and pharmaceutical subsidies—on provincial-level GDP growth across six South Asian emerging economies. Employing a system-GMM dynamic panel estimator with Windmeijer-corrected standard errors to address endogeneity and persistence bias, the analysis reveals that preventive care spending generates a statistically significant multiplier of 1.42 (p < 0.01) over a three-year horizon, substantially exceeding the multiplier associated with curative care infrastructure (0.87). Results are robust to alternative instrument specifications and Hadri panel stationarity diagnostics…

Out-of-Pocket Catastrophic Health Expenditure and Household Poverty Transitions: Micro-Econometric Evidence from the Pakistan Social and Living Standards Measurement Survey

Sana Riaz, Ahmed Raza Cheema, Nadia Tahir
Pakistan Institute of Development Economics (PIDE), Islamabad; Department of Health Economics, University of the Punjab, Lahore
Using three successive waves of the Pakistan Social and Living Standards Measurement (PSLM) survey (2014–2023), this paper estimates the probability of household transitions into multidimensional poverty as a consequence of catastrophic out-of-pocket (OOP) health expenditure. Applying a probit model with Heckman selection correction and instrumental variable strategies to account for non-random health-seeking behavior, we find that households exceeding the 40% threshold of non-food expenditure on healthcare face a 34 percentage-point higher probability of downward poverty transition. Rural households and those headed by informal-sector workers exhibit significantly greater vulnerability…

Keywords: catastrophic health expenditure, poverty transitions, probit, PSLM, OOP payments

Original Research Article

Universal Health Coverage and Allocative Efficiency in Sub-Saharan Africa: A Non-Parametric Frontier Analysis of National Health Insurance Schemes

Oluwaseun Adeyemi, Grace Mwangi
School of Health Systems and Public Health, University of Pretoria, South Africa
This study evaluates the allocative efficiency of national health insurance schemes (NHIS) across 28 Sub-Saharan African nations using Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) combined with a Simar-Wilson bootstrapped truncated regression at the second stage. Health system inputs—total health expenditure per capita, physician density, and hospital bed ratios—are modeled against composite UHC service coverage indices and under-five mortality rates. Findings indicate that only seven countries operate on the efficient frontier, while the mean technical efficiency score stands at 0.68, suggesting a potential 32% improvement in coverage outcomes without additional resource mobilization…
JEL: I11, I18, H42, O55
Keywords: UHC, DEA, allocative efficiency, NHIS, Sub-Saharan Africa
pp. 190–221

Regulatory Impact Assessment of Tobacco Taxation Policies on Smoking Prevalence and Government Revenue: A Structural Vector Autoregressive Approach for OECD Economies

Henrik Johansson, Laura Méndez García
Institute of Health Policy & Management, Erasmus University Rotterdam, The Netherlands; Department of Fiscal Economics, Universidad Complutense de Madrid, Spain
Employing a structural vector autoregressive (SVAR) framework with sign restrictions and Cholesky identification, this paper quantifies the dual impact pathway of ad valorem and specific tobacco excise tax increases on smoking prevalence rates and exchequer revenue across 24 OECD economies (2000–2024). Impulse response functions indicate that a one-standard-deviation positive shock to effective tobacco tax rates reduces age-standardized smoking prevalence by 2.1 percentage points within four years, while net fiscal revenue increases by an estimated 0.3% of GDP in the short run before exhibiting a Laffer-curve trajectory…
